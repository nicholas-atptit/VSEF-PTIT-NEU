# Key Findings

## Finding 1: Governance Worked

VSEF v1 has a frozen scope, model registry, data policy, evaluation policy, and project tracker. This prevents unsupported claims and keeps the system bounded as a research framework.

## Finding 2: Experiment Standardization Worked

One config can generate auditable outputs with manifest, metrics, predictions, and reports. The Phase 1 orchestration pattern makes later evidence easier to inspect and compare.

## Finding 3: Forecasting Models Did Not Consistently Beat Baselines

Simple baselines remain highly competitive on MAE/RMSE. The current evidence does not prove consistent model superiority over baselines.

## Finding 4: Stacking Did Not Prove Superiority

Stacking ran within the forecasting core, but it did not show broad superiority over baselines. It should be treated as runtime and comparison evidence, not proof of ensemble dominance.

## Finding 5: Risk-Aware Ranking Did Not Improve Aggregate Utility

Risk-aware ranking showed selected context-specific improvements, but it did not improve aggregate candidate utility over forecast-only ranking. This points to future work in risk feature design and eligibility gates.

## Finding 6: Regime-Aware Analysis Is the Strongest Contribution

Model behavior, risk-layer utility, and horizon quality depend on market regime under the tested definitions. Phase 4 gives the project its strongest academic framing.

## Finding 7: No Universal Best Model Claim Is Supported

The correct thesis is regime-dependent evaluation, not universal model dominance. VSEF v1 does not prove that any single ML forecasting model dominates all market conditions.

## Final Conclusion

VSEF v1 should be positioned as a governed, reproducible diagnostic research framework for evaluating stock forecasting models, risk-aware candidate policies, and regime-dependent behavior. It should not be positioned as an investment recommendation system.
